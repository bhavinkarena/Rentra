The one decision that matters

Deep field green — because every competitor already owns warm.

Look at the category and one thing jumps out: everybody picked a warm accent. Airbnb's coral, SaffronStays' saffron, YOLO's orange, BookMyFarm's warm red. Pick another orange and Rentra is visually indistinguishable in a Google results page, a WhatsApp forward, or a screenshot someone sends a friend.

Four reasons it is green, not a fifth orange

ReasonWhy it holds
It is native to the subjectFarms and farmland. Green is not an arbitrary brand choice here, it is the colour of the thing being rented — which is the difference between a palette that feels chosen and one that feels picked from a swatch book.
Nobody in the category owns itOn a search results page of orange competitors, one deep green card is the only one a returning visitor recognises without reading.
Green already means “verified, safe, go”Rentra's entire pitch is verified owner, money held safely, no brokerage. The accent colour states the positioning before a word is read.
It leaves red freeAirbnb has a real problem here: their primary action colour is red, so a genuine error state has nowhere distinct to live. Ours does — see semantic colours.
The failure mode to avoid: green tips into “organic produce”, “agri-tech” or “pharmacy” the moment it gets bright or minty. Ours is deliberately deep and slightly desaturated (#2E6449), sitting on a warm near-white rather than a cool grey. That keeps it hospitable instead of clinical. Never use --brand-400 or lighter as a fill behind white text.
This is not the palette from the plan documents. Those use a steel-and-paper industrial look because they are internal reference material for you. This is the product's public language. Do not mix the two — nothing that a Customer or Client sees should use the plan document's colours.
What the category converges on

Seven patterns every reference site shares. Copy them.

These are not trends, they are conventions — and in a trust business, breaking a convention costs conversion without buying distinctiveness. Rentra should be recognisably different in colour and one component, and boringly conventional everywhere else.

The two places Rentra deliberately differs

1 · A slot cell in the search bar

  • Every reference site sells nights, so their search bar is Where / When / Who.
  • Farmhouses in India sell a day picnic and an overnight separately on the same date, at different prices.
  • So Rentra's bar is Where / When / Slot / Guests — and the slot selector is the one component no competitor has.
  • It is also the visible proof of the slot-based availability model in the implementation plan.

2 · A brokerage line in every price block

  • Portals hide fees until a phone call. That is the pain Rentra exists to remove.
  • So the price breakdown always carries a literal Brokerage — ₹0 row, in brand green.
  • It costs one line of markup and it is the entire positioning, rendered.
  • Never suppress it, even when the total is small.

Typography

One family, nine sizes, three scripts.

Airbnb commissioned a bespoke typeface. You cannot, so pick the closest free thing and use it with the same restraint. Plus Jakarta Sans is a geometric-humanist sans with the same friendly, slightly rounded quality as Cereal, and it is on Google Fonts.

Why not Inter: it is the default choice of every generated interface in existence, and it would make Rentra look like a dashboard template. Plus Jakarta Sans costs the same (nothing) and carries actual warmth — which matters when you are asking a stranger to send ₹8,000 for a family outing.

Always font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums on money and dates. Proportional digits make a column of prices look ragged and a countdown timer jitter on every tick. This is one line of CSS and it is the difference between looking built and looking assembled.

Indic scripts — solved in the font stack, not in code

The implementation plan requires the entire Client experience in Gujarati or Hindi. Plus Jakarta Sans has no Devanagari or Gujarati coverage, so the stack does the work: the browser picks the right face per script automatically, with no locale conditionals anywhere in your components.

/* One stack. Latin from Jakarta, Indic from Noto, metrics stay compatible. */
--font-sans: 'Plus Jakarta Sans', 'Noto Sans Devanagari',

Two things Indic scripts break. Devanagari and Gujarati need roughly 15–20% more line-height than Latin at the same size, or the matras clip — set line-height: 1.75 under [lang="hi"] and [lang="gu"]. Components

Rendered, not described. Copy the markup.

Buttons

One primary button per screen. If two things look equally primary, neither is. On a listing page the primary is Book — everything else is secondary or ghost. The WhatsApp green is the one permitted exception to the single-accent rule, because it is a recognised platform colour and users scan for it; keep it secondary in weight and never place it above the Book button.

Inputs

Search without logging in — discovery is never gated.
This farmhouse allows up to 40 guests.

Filter chips

These double as your SEO intent routes — each chip maps to a /[city]/[category]/[intent] landing page. Use aria-pressed, not a class, so screen readers report state.

Badges & rating

✓ Physically Verified Owner Verified Fast Responder ⚡ Instant Book Peak date New 4.8 (23)
Never show more than two badges on one card. Overcrowded trust signals cause cognitive overload and measurably hurt conversion — the test is to remove one at a time and see if conversion drops. Rank them: Physically Verified beats Owner Verified beats everything else. And never render a rating badge before real reviews exist — an empty “★ 0.0 (0)” is worse than no badge, and fake structured-data ratings get sites penalised.

Slot selector — Rentra's own component

Maps directly to availability(rentable_id, date, slot). Selecting Full day consumes both slots. Show the price for each slot inline once a date is picked — the price gap between day and night is the reason this component exists.

The rules

Read this before you add anything to the system.

Always

  • One primary button per screen. Everything else is secondary or ghost.
  • Green means act or confirmed. Nothing else may be brand green.
  • Tabular numerals on every price, date and timer.
  • Show the number, not the policy. “You get ₹9,140 back” beats a refund table.
  • The ₹0 brokerage line in every price breakdown, without exception.
  • Real photos. The system is deliberately quiet so photography can carry it. Bad photos will break this design more than any code will.
  • Mobile first, tested on a mid-range Android, because that is where a forwarded WhatsApp link opens.
  • Test the Client side in Gujarati before shipping, not after.

Never

  • Never add a second accent colour. Two accents means no accent. WhatsApp green is the single permitted exception.
  • Never use amber for a button or brand green for a warning.
  • Never put a border, shadow and radius on everything. Each says “separate object”; spend them by role or the hierarchy flattens.
  • Never show more than two badges on a card, or an empty “★ 0.0 (0)” rating.
  • Never fake a strike-through price. A permanent fake discount makes you a coupon site.
  • Never gate search behind login. It is the behaviour people hate about the existing portals and it kills your SEO.
  • Never use the plan documents' steel-and-paper palette in the product.
  • Never fix a button's width. Gujarati and Hindi strings run 20–40% longer.
How to extend this system. When you need something that is not here, do not invent a token — compose from what exists. If you genuinely cannot (say, Phase 3 needs a state for “goods in transit”), add one token, write down its single job in this file, and check its contrast before you use it. A design system dies by a hundred small additions, each individually reasonable.

The whole system in one line: a white page, near-black text, one deep green for anything that acts, amber only for stars, and photography doing all the rest of the work.

What makes it Rentra and not a clone: the green in a category of orange, the slot cell in the search bar, and a ₹0 brokerage line in every price block. Everything else is deliberately conventional, because in a trust business a surprising interface is a liability.
