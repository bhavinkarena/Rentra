The three rules that make the sequence work:

FOUNDATIONS · DECIDE ONCE, BEFORE PHASE 1

The four decisions everything else hangs on

These are not build tasks. They are positions you take before the first commit, because each one is either free now or a migration later.

1 · The promise is narrow, the name is broad

Market yourself as “rent anything” and the idea is a 5/10 — four sets of owners, four pricing models, four legal flows, zero bookings. Market yourself as “book a verified farmhouse in your district, transparent price, money held safely, no broker” and it is an 8–9/10, because that is a door you can actually open.

But name the company for where it ends up. Rentra carries no category in it. A name like RentSpace forces a re-brand at exactly the moment you can least afford it — mid-expansion, with SEO equity, WhatsApp templates, an Instagram handle and 120 owner relationships attached to the old word.

Position: broad name, narrow homepage. Register rentra.in, the social handles and the trademark classes for both rental of immovable property and rental of goods now, while it costs nothing. Until Phase 3 the homepage says farmhouses in your district and nothing else.

2 · The rental quadrant — where every future category lands

Two axes, not one. Short-versus-long is the obvious axis. The one that decides your architecture is: does the renter travel to the thing, or does the thing travel to the renter? Cross them and every category you will ever consider lands in exactly one box — each with its own availability object, money flow, paperwork and dominant risk. Those four differences are precisely the things that are expensive to retrofit.

Two conclusions the quadrant hands you. Mode D is an asset-heavy lending business wearing a rental costume — you buy the stock, carry the depreciation and underwrite the customer's credit. It shares a website with your marketplace and nothing else; if you want it, start it later as a different company. And Mode C should overtake Mode B in the roadmap: Mode B is the quadrant where you cannot hold the money, cannot take a clean commission, and lose the customer after one transaction, while Mode C is where you hold the full amount and the same customer comes back for every function.

One flag, not a fifth mode: requires_operator. In India rented goods usually arrive with a person — a sound system with an operator, a generator with a technician, a tempo traveller with a driver. That changes pricing, liability and verification. Make it a boolean on the asset with an operator-charge field, not a separate flow.

3 · The schema — three columns that prevent a rewrite LOCK

The shortest, most load-bearing decision in this document. If your first migration creates a properties table with a calendar, every future category becomes a schema fight. Name the core object for what it is — a rentable thing — and add three columns you will not use for eighteen months.

-- The core object. Not "properties". Everything you will ever rent lives here.
rentable (
  id, owner_id, title, slug, description, status,

  -- COLUMN 1 · fixed or movable. Decides the whole fulfilment flow.
  form              enum('fixed','movable'),

  -- COLUMN 2 · how possession transfers. 'visit_site' is a VALUE, not an absence.
  fulfilment        enum('visit_site','pickup_from_owner','delivered'),

  -- COLUMN 3 · how time is sold. One enum, not per-category hardcoding.
  rental_unit       enum('slot','night','day','week','month'),

  category_id,       -- farmhouse | lawn | chairs | tempo_traveller | dslr ...
  city_id, area_id,  -- drives the SEO route tree, see below
  requires_operator  bool,
  total_units        int      -- 1 for a farmhouse. 800 for a tent-house's chairs.
)

-- Availability is units-over-time, which degrades correctly to a calendar.
-- Farmhouse: total_units = 1, so "1 unit consumed" == "that slot is blocked".
-- Chairs:    total_units = 800, so 40 consumed still leaves 760 bookable.
availability (rentable_id, date, slot, units_available, price_override)
   UNIQUE (rentable_id, date, slot)   <- the lock that prevents double-booking

-- Serial-numbered physical items. Movable goods only; empty for places.
unit (id, rentable_id, serial_no, condition_grade, status)

booking (
  id, rentable_id, seeker_id, starts_at, ends_at, slot,
  units_booked        int,
  amount_rent, amount_fee, amount_deposit,
  amount_advance_paid,    -- part-payment is the Indian norm; see Phase 1
  balance_mode         enum('online_before','cash_on_arrival','none'),
  state               enum('requested','confirmed','handed_over',
                           'returned','completed','cancelled','disputed')
)

-- Payout fields exist from day one even if the values are zero.
-- Retrofitting tax deduction onto a live payout pipeline is genuinely painful.
payout (booking_id, gross, commission, tds_194o, gst_tcs, net, status, utr)

Note the state machine. A person checking into a farmhouse and a camera leaving a shop are the same seven states — handed_over and returned describe both. Check-in is the handover of a place. Nothing else needs to change to support goods later.

Cost of doing this now: about half a day of naming discipline. Cost of doing it in year two: rewriting the booking engine, the availability lock, the search index, the pricing calculator and every admin screen, while paying customers use the old one.

4 · Roles — three, and one account is one role LOCK

Every action in the product belongs to one of three roles. An account carries exactly one role. A Client who wants to rent something as a Customer opens a separate Customer account — roles never mix on one login, and there is no role switcher anywhere in the product.

The three roles — for every phase
RoleWho they areWhat they do
CustomerThe person rentingSearches, compares, asks questions in masked chat, books and pays, checks in, checks out, reviews.
Client
owner · authorised agent		The person listingKYC + ownership proof, creates listings, sets price and calendar, accepts bookings, hosts check-in and check-out, receives payout.
Super AdminYouKYC approval, listing moderation, chat-flag review, dispute decisions, payouts and refunds, owner recruitment — and field verification, which is a task you perform, not a role you build a screen for.

What single-role means in the schema

Three consequences, each cheap to handle now and painful later.

-- Customers and Clients share auth, sessions, notifications and chat,
-- so they share one table. The role is fixed at signup and never changes.
user (
  id, phone, name, email,
  role          enum('customer','client'),   -- immutable after creation
  person_id     NULL,  -- optional link between the same human's two accounts
  client_type   enum('owner','authorised_agent') NULL,  -- Clients only
  ...
)
   UNIQUE (phone, role)   <- NOT unique on phone alone

-- Super Admin is a different table with different auth. No self-signup,
-- no public login route, and never a 'role' value on the user table.
admin_user (id, email, password_hash, totp_secret, name, last_login_at)

-- One verified human, created from the KYC vendor's result.
-- Lets you reuse a completed verification across that person's two accounts.
person (id, kyc_ref, verified_name, verified_at)
  • Uniqueness is on (phone, role), not on phone. A Client who wants to book someone else's farmhouse for his son's birthday has only one phone number. If phone is globally unique he simply cannot sign up as a Customer, and you will discover this from a support call rather than from a test.
  • Separate login entry points, since the role cannot be inferred: /login for Customers, /partner/login for Clients. This is a benefit — the two onboardings share almost nothing, and the partner side gets its own landing page to rank for “list your farmhouse”.
  • Link the two accounts by person_id so KYC is done once. Same human, two logins, one verification — otherwise you pay the KYC vendor twice and ask a verified owner to photograph his Aadhaar again, which is exactly the friction that loses him.
Every later role is a child of Client, not a fourth role. The caretaker in Phase 2 becomes client_staff(client_id, phone, permissions). The vendor business in Phase 3 becomes another client_type value with inventory tables hanging off it. The logistics partner is the same. That keeps the role count at three permanently, which is the real payoff of this decision — the permission model never grows a fourth branch.
The field verifier is your real moat, and the caretaker is the reason your dispute process will or will not work. Anyone can copy a website; nobody copies a person who has physically stood on all 60 farms in your district. And every operational step this plan assigns to the Client — check-in, condition photos, inspection — is physically done by the caretaker, because the owner lives in the city and visits on Sundays. Budget ₹300–₹800 per verification visit and treat it as acquisition cost, not overhead.
FOUNDATIONS · SEO ARCHITECTURE

Decide the URL tree before you write a single route LOCK

For a rental marketplace, organic search is a primary acquisition channel, not an optimisation. Somebody typing “farmhouse on rent in Kamrej” is the cheapest customer you will ever get, and they compound for years. But URL structure is the one SEO decision you cannot redo cheaply — every change costs redirects, lost rankings and a re-crawl. So it belongs here, in the foundations, not in a later optimisation pass.

The route tree

The rule: anything you want indexed lives in the path; anything you do not lives in a query string. City, category, area and search intent are path segments because each one deserves its own ranking page. Price, guest count and dates are query parameters because you never want them indexed.

RouteWhat it isRenderingIndexed
/Home — city chooser, trust proof, top listingsStaticYes
/[city]/surat — city landing, all categoriesISR 1hYes
/[city]/[category]/surat/farmhouse — the money pageISR 1hYes
/[city]/[category]/[area]/surat/farmhouse/kamrejISR 1hIf ≥3 listings
/[city]/[category]/[intent]/surat/farmhouse/day-picnic, /with-pool, /for-pre-wedding-shootISR 1hIf ≥3 listings
/listing/[slug]-[id]The listing detail pageISR + on-demandYes
/search?…Dated, filtered resultsDynamicNo — noindex, follow
/guide/[slug]Real editorial: how to pick a farmhouse, price guidesStaticYes
/(app)/…Booking, chat, dashboard, owner, adminDynamicNo
Three traps to avoid on day one. Facet explosion: never let every filter combination generate an indexable URL — whitelist the combinations worth a page and send everything else noindex, follow with a canonical to the parent. Thin pages: an area page with zero listings is a doorway page and Google will treat your whole site accordingly, so auto-noindex any landing page under three live listings and auto-flip it to indexed when it crosses. Dead listings: when a listing is delisted, never leave a 404 wall — 301 it to its area page and keep the link equity.

Ops & instrumentation

Do not build in Phase 1

  • Masked chat. You are the chat. Build it once you have seen what people actually ask.
  • Escrow, the dispute engine, the trust-score algorithm, automated payouts. All of it after you have watched 30 bookings by hand.

Why farmhouses, and who you are competing with PART 1 §1–2

“Rent anything” is not workable as a first product. Four categories on day one needs four sets of owners, four pricing models and four legal flows, all before a single booking. But the pain is real: in India most rental discovery still runs through brokers, watchmen, WhatsApp groups and “my cousin knows someone,” and people pay one to two months' rent as brokerage for information that should be free.

Cancellation & refund tiers PART 1 §12

The owner picks one tier when listing. It is displayed in plain language and the refund is computed automatically — never negotiated in chat.

Seeker-side tiers (illustrative — tune with real data)
Tier7+ days before3–7 daysUnder 48 hrsNo-show
Flexible100% rent100% rent50% rentNone
Moderate (default)100% rent50% rentNoneNone
Strict (peak dates)50% rentNoneNoneNone
  • The security deposit is always refunded in full on cancellation, in every tier. It is not a penalty instrument.
  • The platform fee is refundable on flexible-tier full cancellations and on any owner-side cancellation; otherwise retained. Say so at checkout.
  • Owner cancels a confirmed booking: seeker gets 100% back plus a platform credit, dates auto-block, listing de-ranks temporarily, repeated cancellations lead to suspension. Price this painfully — owner-side cancellation is the fastest way to destroy a rental marketplace's reputation.
  • On-arrival mismatch reported within 60 minutes with photos is treated as an owner cancellation.
  • Force majeure (flood, bandh, curfew, government order): full refund, no owner penalty. Write it into the terms so you are not improvising during the next monsoon.

AreaWhat to handlePhase
Business entityPrivate Limited once you handle other people's money or plan to raise. An LLP works bootstrapped. Either way you need a current account and gateway onboarding.1
Marketplace positioningTerms must state clearly that you are an intermediary facilitating a transaction, not the owner, lessor or operator of any property. This matters enormously if an accident happens at a property. Never describe yourself as an operator, ever.1
Booking terms per transactionAuto-generate a short agreement per confirmed booking — duration, capacity, house rules, damage liability, cancellation — accepted by tick-box with timestamp and IP log. This is your evidence in any dispute.1
Holding depositsUse the payment aggregator's escrow and settlement mechanism. Holding customer deposits in your own account puts you in RBI payment-aggregator territory you do not want to be in. Confirm the maximum hold window before designing the money flow.1
Data protection (DPDP Act)Aadhaar and PAN data is sensitive personal data. Store verification results and vendor reference IDs, not raw ID images. Publish a privacy policy, collect consent, define retention periods.1
IT Rules — intermediary safe harbourPublish a grievance officer's name and contact, respond within statutory timelines, keep a takedown process for fraudulent listings.1
Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) RulesMandatory display of supplier details, published grievance timelines, no misleading representation, clear refund process. Your automatic refund engine already puts you ahead — just make sure the policy shown at checkout is the policy the code applies.1–2
GSTRegistration mandatory past the threshold and effectively needed earlier to invoice commission properly. GST applies to your commission; whether it applies to the rent depends on property type and the owner's own registration. Accommodation and place rental have their own rate structure. One CA view per quadrant, before you launch that quadrant.1–2
TDS — section 194-OAn e-commerce operator deducts tax at source on the gross amount credited to a seller or service provider through its platform. Rate reduced to 0.1% with effect from October 2024; a higher rate applies with no PAN. Applies from your first commercial payout, not from a turnover threshold. Build the field into the payout record now.2
TCS under GST §52An e-commerce operator collecting consideration for suppliers collects tax at source and files monthly (GSTR-8). Ask your CA specifically whether collecting the rent and remitting to the owner makes you an ECO — the answer shapes whether you collect the rent at all or only the fee.2
InsuranceDamage protection only through a licensed insurer as partner. Never informally promise “we'll cover it” — that is unlimited liability on your balance sheet. Places need premises liability; movable goods need goods-in-transit and equipment breakdown; vehicles need a commercial motor policy.2–3
Local permissionsFarmhouses hosting events may need local body permission, noise compliance and fire safety. You are not liable for the owner's compliance, but your terms should require them to declare it — and you should ask during verification.1
Motor Vehicles Act — the vehicle blockerA privately registered vehicle cannot legally be rented out. Self-drive requires registration for that use — historically the Rent-a-Cab Scheme 1989, or a state self-drive or aggregator scheme — with commercial insurance. Motorcycles sit under separate state schemes. This kills peer-to-peer vehicle rental in India: the Turo model is not available to you. Vehicles arrive as licensed operators.3–4
GST on renting goodsRenting movable goods is a supply of service, generally 18%; renting with an operator can be treated differently. Do not assume the farmhouse treatment carries over to chairs.3
Movement of goodsMoving goods above a value threshold generally requires a delivery challan and, where applicable, an e-way bill — including for movement that is not a sale. Another reason the handover challan should be platform-generated: it does double duty as the compliance document and the evidence document.3
Hypothecated assetsFinanced goods are typically hypothecated to the lender, and loan terms often bar commercial use or sub-letting without consent. Add a declaration to vendor onboarding rather than discovering it after a claim.3
Rent agreements (Mode B)Integrate an e-stamp and e-sign partner rather than generating unstamped agreements. Stamp duty varies by state; police verification of tenants is required in several cities.4
This is general information, not legal advice. Before launch, spend one paid session with a CA and one with a lawyer who has worked with marketplaces — it costs a fraction of what a wrong structure costs to unwind. Two rules of thumb: anything touching a payout is expensive to retrofit, so build the tax fields now even at zero; and never launch a new quadrant without one paid CA hour on that quadrant's GST and TDS treatment, because each quadrant genuinely differs.

Risks & mitigations PART 1 §21 · PART 2
RiskMitigation
Off-platform bypass — they meet once, deal directly next timeMasking pre-booking, fair commission (8–12%), value only the platform provides (calendar, deposit handling, reviews, dispute support), loyalty slabs both sides. Accept some leakage as structural; never respond by raising fees.
Fake listings, brokers posing as ownersMandatory KYC + ownership document + physical or video verification before going live; reverse-image checks; report button; suspension on the first confirmed fake.
Property damage or unruly guestsDeposit sized to the property, seeker KYC above a threshold, condition photos both ends, capacity enforced in the booking, insurance partner for high-value venues.
Owner cancels last minuteHeavy penalty, temporary de-ranking, platform credit, and the instant-rebook guarantee as the ops answer.
Double-bookingDatabase-level locking on (rentable, date, slot), effortless offline-block for owners, and the override policy only as a last resort.
Stale listings, unresponsive ownersAuto-hide after 30 days without confirmation, one-tap reactivate over WhatsApp, response rate shown publicly and used in ranking.
Seasonality — monsoon, exams, off-peak weekdaysWeekday and off-season pricing tools, corporate offsite and shoot-location demand to fill weekdays, event spaces as a partial counter-cycle.
Low liquidity at launchOne district, manual owner recruitment, founder-led first 20 bookings, requirement broadcast, and no demand spend until supply is deep.
Payment and regulatory exposure on depositsGateway escrow and split payments from day one; never hold customer money in your own account; confirm the hold window in advance.
Chargeback fraud on a young platformVelocity limits, first-booking value ceiling, strict KYC on cards, no same-day payout on a new owner's first booking, UPI preferred at high value.
Liability if something happens at a propertyClear intermediary positioning in ToS, per-booking terms, owner declarations on local permissions, insurance partner. Never describe yourself as an operator.
Non-return of goods (Phase 3)Vendor-only supply, address-verified renters, deposits scaled to asset value, and the platform-generated signed handover challan that makes a police complaint actionable.
A funded competitor copies youYour defensibility is not the software — it is district-level supply depth, verified inventory and owner relationships. Those take feet on the ground and cannot be bought quickly. Go deep in one belt before going wide.

Bottom line: “Rent anything” is a 5/10 idea. “Book a verified farmhouse in your district online, at a transparent price, with your money held safely and no broker in the middle” is an 8–9/10 idea — and it is the same platform, aimed at a door you can actually open. Prove it manually with 30 bookings. Then build the machine that does it 3,000 times.

The three things to get right before you write application code: the schema names (rentable, not properties; form / fulfilment / rental_unit), the URL tree, and the slot-based availability key. Half a day of discipline against a rewrite in year two.

Positioning — don't say: “A marketplace to rent anything.” Say: “Find and book a place directly from the owner — verified, transparent, no brokerage.”
