CLIENT SIDE · THE OWNER WHO LISTS A PROPERTY

A funnel with no destination

Every Client screen that exists today is about getting a property in: sign up, prove who you are, fill nine sections, submit. It is well built. Then the listing goes live — and the product stops. There is no screen that answers the only three questions an owner opens Rentra to ask after his first week.

THE SHORT VERSION

Three questions, none of them answerable

Week one, an owner is building a listing, and the product is excellent at that. Week two onwards he opens Rentra for one of three reasons, and every one of them lands on a page that does not exist.

“Did I get a booking?”

There is no booking inbox. The booking table has a state machine, an acceptDeadline and a checkInCode column — and no Client surface reads any of it. A request would arrive and sit there.

“Am I free on Saturday?”

There is no calendar. availability.blockedByClient exists precisely so an owner can take a weekend out for a family function. Nothing writes to it. The site will keep selling dates he has already given away.

“When do I get paid?”

There is no earnings or payout screen. The payout table already carries gross, commission, TDS, TCS, net, status and UTR. Every one of those is invisible to the person the money belongs to.

The good news, and it is genuinely good: almost none of this needs a migration. The schema was written for the whole owner lifecycle and then only the first third was built on top of it. Six tables and roughly a dozen columns are sitting there fully designed with nothing pointed at them. Most of what follows is screens and Server Actions, not database work.

The one thing that stops everything: deliverOtp throws in production — no SMS or email provider is wired. Today, on a production deployment, no owner can log in at all. That is item one of phase C0 and it should be done this week, because WhatsApp template approval takes days per language and everything in C1 and C4 waits on it.

C1 The booking inbox2–3 weeks · the missing centre of the product ▸

The moment a booking request can exist, the owner needs somewhere to answer it. This is the phase that turns a listing directory into a marketplace, and it is the single largest gap on the Client side.

GATE TO EXIT → A request is accepted from a phone in under thirty seconds, and a request nobody answers expires, refunds in full and reopens the slot with no human involved.
requested→ confirmed→ handed_over→ returned→ completed cancelled disputed

All seven states are already in the schema. The Client can currently move a booking through none of them.

  • /partner/bookings

    Requests first, then upcoming, then past. The requests list is the entire reason the screen exists — everything else is reference.

  • Accept and decline Server Actions

    Accepting consumes the availability row inside the same transaction, so Postgres refuses the double booking rather than the UI. The composite primary key is already the lock; the action just has to let it work.

    availability(rentable_id, day, slot) — primary key
  • The 12-hour expiry sweep

    A worker job on acceptDeadline: full refund, slot reopened, no questions asked, and a note against the owner’s response rate. The index for the query already exists.

    booking_state_deadline_idx · worker/
  • /partner/bookings/[reference]

    Guest badge, past bookings and review history first; dates, guests and money second. An owner decides on the person, not on the amount — leading with the amount is how a platform trains an owner to accept guests he should not.

  • Contact unlock at confirmation

    Exact address, both phone numbers and the check-in code become visible the moment the state flips — not a minute earlier, and hidden again seven days after check-out.

    booking.checkInCode · rentable.exactAddress
  • Populate the response signals

    Median minutes to respond and the share of requests answered before expiry. Both columns exist and are null on every row; they are what a Fast Responder badge is made of.

    user.responds_within_mins · user.response_rate
  • Structured decline reasons

    Booked elsewhere, price too low, guest profile, dates unsuitable. De-ranking an owner for the first of those is punishing him for a calendar you have not built yet.

  • Push the request to WhatsApp

    With accept and decline as links. Twelve hours is not a browser window, it is a WhatsApp window. This is also the first real test of the template pipeline started in C0.

  • Owner-side cancellation, with the penalty visible before confirming

    ownerCancellationRefund() already returns full refund plus a penalty flag. Show the owner what it costs him at the moment he is deciding, not after.

    lib/domain/pricing.js

    This month, last month, per listing. Gross, commission, tax, net — the same four numbers every time, in the same order. This is the screen that decides whether he lists his second property.

    One row per payout with its status and UTR. A pending payout that says nothing is an inbound call; a pending payout that says “settles Tuesday” is not.

    Today the holder name the owner typed is compared to his KYC name. A real penny-drop returns the name the bank holds. Paying out to a third-party account is how a marketplace becomes a laundering route.

  • Deposit lifecycle in the owner’s view

    Held, claim window open, auto-refunded, claimed. The deposit is always returned in full on a cancellation regardless of tier — the refund function already enforces that, so say so on screen and stop the argument before it starts.

  • Tax, from the first rupee

    TDS and TCS on every payout row plus a downloadable quarterly statement. The columns exist at zero by design; confirm the rates with a CA and start writing real numbers into them before volume, not after.

    lib/domain/pricing.js — PLATFORM_FEE_RATE is a constant today
  • No same-day payout on a first booking

    Stolen card in, payout out, chargeback lands forty days later and you pay both sides. A hold on a new owner’s first settlement costs one uncomfortable conversation and prevents the whole class of loss.

    The dashboard already quotes the ₹8,000–₹14,500 band to a signed-in owner. That number belongs before the signup, on the page a forwarded WhatsApp link opens.

    app/(partner)/partner/page.js
C5 Operations on the ground~4 weeks · the caretaker, the evidence, the numbers ▸

Every operational step the plan assigns to the owner is physically performed by someone else — the caretaker who unlocks the gate, shows the guests around and takes the cash. Until that person has an account, the photo requirement the whole dispute process rests on will simply not be complied with.

GATE TO EXIT → Caretaker completes check-in on 80% of bookings, owner-side cancellation under 2%, disputes under 3 per 100 bookings and each one closed with written reasoning shown to both sides.
  • /partner/staff — caretaker sub-accounts

    Phone login, scoped permissions: can check guests in, can capture condition photos, can mark a return, can confirm cash — cannot see earnings, cannot change prices. Build the primitive once; it serves the caretaker now and the vendor’s delivery driver in Phase 3.

    client_staff — table and permissions jsonb already exist
  • Condition proof capture

    In-app camera only, gallery blocked, timestamped and geotagged, at check-in and at check-out, with a side-by-side comparison. The authenticated upload path already exists in the document vault.

    lib/uploads/cloudinary.js · lib/auth/documents.js
  • Check-in code verification

    A single screen for the person standing at the gate. checkInCode is already a column on booking.

  • Damage claim with an evidence window

    Claim, automatic deposit freeze, a fixed 48-hour window, an admin decision, and the same written reasoning shown to both sides. Transparency here is what stops a dispute becoming a public complaint.

    bookingState 'disputed' · payoutStatus 'frozen'
  • Reply to a guest review

    One unanswered bad review costs more churn than three good ones win. Reviews release only when both sides submit or after 14 days — the reply is separate from that and should stay separate.

  • /partner/insights

    Views, enquiries, requests, confirmed — per listing, as a funnel rather than page views. An owner who cannot see why he is not getting bookings decides the platform is dead and stops confirming availability, which makes it true.

  • Surface the verification visit

    When it is, whether it is a video call or a physical visit, who is coming and what they will check. The owner has to be at the property and is currently told none of it.

    verification_visit.scheduled_at · mode · assigned_to
  • A walkthrough video step in the builder

    It is what the guest-side plan already assumes exists, and the cheapest possible input to video verification.

  • Bulk actions across listings

    Block the same week on all four farmhouses at once. Only worth building after the calendar exists, which is why it sits here and not in C2.

RESTRAINT

Do not build these on the Client side

An owner mobile app

He arrives through a forwarded WhatsApp link and opens it in a browser. Mobile web camera capture is good enough for condition proof until there is real volume.

A composite trust score

Show the raw signals — completed bookings, response time, cancellations, rating. They are more convincing precisely because nobody has to trust your weighting.

Channel sync or an ICS feed

Real for an owner also listed elsewhere, and genuinely useful — but not before there are enough listings for the sync to be worth maintaining.

Custom cancellation policies

Three tiers, computed, never negotiated in chat. A per-owner policy is a support queue with extra steps, and the refund function is deliberately one function.

Featured or boosted listings

Meaningless until listing density makes ranking worth paying for.

Bulk CSV listing import

An owner with twenty properties is not the Phase 2 customer, and the ownership document check does not survive a bulk import anyway — which is the whole point of the check.
